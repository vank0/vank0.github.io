Flame ring — interval counter

The counter circle from the interval view, wrapped in fire. The flame appears only when a heart-rate sensor is streaming, and its size, speed, spark count and colour all follow the live zone — so the effect carries the same information the zone badge does, rather than being decoration that happens to be on fire, and it eases between zones rather than snapping — 148 bpm and 152 bpm are the same effort, so a 4-beat drift should not look like an event. Pick a zone or drag the bpm slider; “No sensor” is the real off state, and the fire fades out rather than vanishing, because a dropped sensor is not a button press. While a zone change is in flight the flicker holds still and resumes once it settles — two motions at once in the same object and the eye reads neither. Trim sets how loud the whole effect is — the zones keep their relative shape, so agreeing one number settles all five.

Two variants — pick one

Same shader, different bias. Everything else is identical.

A · Halo

Fire hugs the ring the whole way round, evenly. Reads as “the circle itself is burning”. Calmer, symmetrical, and it never crowds the label above the ring.

B · Updraft

Flames rise upward off the ring the way real fire does — closest to the library’s own look. More dramatic and more alive; it does eat vertical space above the circle.

What I changed from the library, and why

It needs an API iPhones don’t have

flame-wrap draws your DOM inside the canvas via the experimental HTML‑in‑Canvas API (drawElement). That is Chrome‑only and behind a flag. On iOS Safari — a phone propped against a bench, which is where intervals actually happen — it would render nothing at all. This preview keeps the look and puts the flame on a canvas behind the ring instead, so the digits stay ordinary DOM.

No heat shimmer on the numbers

Distorting the content is the library’s signature trick. Here the content is a countdown you read while gasping, so the shimmer stays on the ring and never touches the digits. The clock is the one thing on screen that must never be hard to read.

The flame is the zone

Colour follows the existing Karvonen ramp — Z1 blue, Z2 green, Z3 yellow, Z4 orange, Z5 red — the same colours the badge and the charts already use. Height, speed and sparks ramp with it, so “more fire” and “higher zone” are the same sentence.

It stops when nothing is watching

The loop runs only with a live bpm, pauses on a hidden tab, and drops to a still glow under prefers-reduced-motion. A WebGL loop during a screen‑locked workout is a battery decision as much as a visual one.
